Integrations

This is where self-driving happens. The engine reads from the tools your company already uses, and when something needs doing, it does it in those same tools.

Who is driving

You decide how much it does by itself

Reading is automatic. Acting is not: every action falls into one of three groups, and you choose which actions go where.

It prepares, you approve

How everything starts

The engine drafts the email, fills in the issue, or lines up the message, and puts it in your Focus. Nothing leaves until you press the button.

It acts within rules you set

When you trust a kind of action

For actions you have seen it get right, you can let it go ahead: create the issue, post the routine update. You hear about it as a signal afterward, and can turn the rule off at any time.

It always asks

Whatever the rules say

Anything that reaches people outside your company, changes who has access to something, or cannot be undone waits for a person, every time.

What keeps an action safe

Only actions on a fixed list
The engine can do what is in its catalog of actions and nothing else. An AI model never invents a new kind of action.
Never more than you could do yourself
An action runs with the permissions of the person who approved it or whose rule allowed it. Ordinary code checks that, not a model.
Checked afterward
After acting, the engine confirms the result in the other tool and updates what it knows. If it did not work, you are told.
On the record
Every action is recorded: what was done, in which tool, on whose approval or under which rule.
